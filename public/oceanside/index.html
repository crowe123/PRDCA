Oceanside Pool Removal Permit Rules

Oceanside publishes no pool-specific demolition handout. Informational Bulletin IB 116, based on the 2025 California codes, quotes the building code's rule that anyone who demolishes a building or structure must first obtain a permit, lists no exemption for pool removal, and notes that Building Division staff will not confirm an exemption verbally. The city's building fee schedule for 2026-27 lists a demolition permit at $31.84 for plan check and $252.71 for inspection, $284.55 in total.

The Development Services Department, Building Division is the office to call before any work starts. Oceanside is one of 11 cities among the 32 in this directory that publish nothing specific to pools, so the department's answer on the phone decides the permit type, the fee and the fill standard.

Soils reports and air district notice in Oceanside

Oceanside's Information Bulletin 108 requires a geotechnical report for new pools and wherever a grading plan is required. It says nothing about removing a pool, so the grading question decides whether a removal needs one.

The San Diego County Air Pollution Control District requires a demolition notification whenever a load-bearing structure is wrecked, and an asbestos survey before the building permit. The district does not say whether a pool shell counts, so the Building Division confirms it with the permit.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Oceanside

No city document sets drainage holes, shell depth or fill rules for pools, so the Building Division decides what Oceanside accepts. The city's grading code does set general standards for permitted controlled fill: layers no thicker than 6 inches, compacted to at least 90% of maximum density. Grading outside the code's exemptions needs a permit from the City Engineer, and a minor grading plan carries a $300 administrative fee, so the depth of fill on a full removal decides whether Engineering is involved.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

Hauling the debris in Oceanside

Oceanside's construction and demolition requirements call for a Waste Management Plan before permits are issued and a signed affidavit at final inspection that the diversion target was met. The plan form sets a minimum of 65% recycled or salvaged under CALGreen, lists demolition among its project types and counts a Waste Management roll-off bin as one way to comply; Waste Management is the city's franchise service provider. The city code requires all debris removed within one week of the work finishing.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Oceanside's pool draining tips say no permit is needed to drain a pool, but the water must test below 1 part per million of chlorine, and the city suggests leaving it in the sun for at least three days to get there. Filter backwash goes to the sewer, not the street, saltwater pools need total dissolved solids below 500 ppm before draining to the street, and the Urban Runoff Hotline is 760-435-4500.

Sellers in Oceanside should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.
