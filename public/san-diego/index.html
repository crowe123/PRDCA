A contractor's licence classes matter on a pool job. San Diego permits are generally issued to holders of the A (general engineering), C-12 (earthwork and paving), C-21 (building moving and demolition) or C-53 (swimming pool) classes described in the state licence board's classification guide, and the accepted classes differ from city to city. Industry Bulletin 24-07 confirms that the $1,000 unlicensed-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit, so a pool removal always needs a licensed contractor or an owner-builder permit.

San Diego Pool Removal Permit Rules

San Diego's Development Services Department issues a Demolition/Removal Permit for pools, described in Information Bulletin 710. The published fees in September 2026 were $360.36 for plan check and $332.48 for inspection, about $693 in total before any other charges. A soils compaction report is required for any pool deeper than five feet. Owner-builders may pull the permit themselves, and the bulletin names the A and C-21 licence classes for contractors.

Partial fill-in or full removal in San Diego

San Diego permits both routes under the same Demolition/Removal Permit. Deep pools, which most older family pools are at the deep end, trigger the soils compaction report either way, so the engineering cost sits in most San Diego jobs. A full removal with compacted fill is the route that keeps the yard buildable for a later structure.

A partial fill-in breaks drainage holes through the floor, removes the top of the wall and fills the shell with soil. The method costs less and finishes faster, usually in one to four days, but most cities limit the area to landscaping afterwards. A full removal hauls away every piece of the shell and replaces it with fill compacted in layers to 90%, which takes three to seven days and is the route for anyone planning a structure over the old pool.

Published prices for San Diego County

San Diego's two published price lists disagree by a factor of three or more. Demo Diego quotes $5,000 to $9,000 for a partial fill-in and $9,000 to $15,000 for a full removal, while Villa Demolition quotes $12,000 to $30,000 and $45,000 to $60,000. A written quote from more than one contractor is the only reliable way to place a pool inside that spread.

Four things move a quote more than anything else: equipment access (about $2,000 more where the side yard is under 6 ft wide), deck removal ($500 to $1,700), fill dirt (80 to 150 cubic yards for a full removal) and the engineer's compaction report ($1,500 to $4,000). The California pool removal cost guide sets out every published price by region and pool type.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The San Diego pools and spas fact sheet and LA Stormwater both allow the storm drain only for dechlorinated water within a set pH range, and San Diego contractors generally follow the same practice. The Development Services Department confirms the local rule.

Removing a pool can lower the assessed value of a San Diego property. The Board of Equalization's letter 86/09 treats removal of an improvement as a reason to reduce assessed value, but the reduction is tied to the pool's own assessed value, so an older pool may bring only a small change. Sellers should also expect the question on the state's seller disclosure form, which asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.
