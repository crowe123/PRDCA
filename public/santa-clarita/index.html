Santa Clarita Pool Removal Permit Rules

Santa Clarita's Building and Safety Division requires a permit whether a pool is filled in place or removed completely, and its pool abandonment bulletin (revised January 2020) sets out the abandonment route. A building permit can be issued over the counter once the application is complete and two site plans, on paper at least 11 by 17 inches, show the property lines, adjacent streets and pool dimensions. The city's 2026-27 fee schedule has no pool line; its general building permit fees are $38 for issuance and $95 for work valued up to $5,000.

Inspections and paperwork on a Santa Clarita job

The site plan also notes that pool equipment is removed and the electrical, water and gas lines are capped. The bulletin then sets two inspections:

  1. A pre-fill inspection of the drainage holes and the broken bottom, before any fill goes in.
  2. A final inspection confirming the pool was filled.

At the final, the inspector signs the inspection card, which the bulletin describes as the property owner's record of the work, so owners should keep it with the property papers.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Santa Clarita

The bulletin's catch is in its wording: a pool filled in place leaves the property permanently recorded as having an abandoned structure, and the pool may have to be removed completely if a future structure is proposed on or near it. Filling in place needs at least two 24-inch holes with the bottom broken up for drainage, the bond beam cut at least 12 inches below grade, and the fill named on the plan as gravel, sand or compacted earth. No compaction report is required unless a future structure is proposed.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

Hauling the debris in Santa Clarita

Santa Clarita's construction and demolition recycling ordinance covers all demolition projects and requires at least 65% of inert materials and 65% of everything else recycled. A Construction and Demolition Materials Management Plan and a refundable deposit of 10% or $15,000, whichever is less, are due before the building permit, and proof of diversion within 180 days of the final inspection. Bins and roll-offs must come from one of the city's franchised haulers, which include Burrtec and Waste Management, and contractors may self-haul only under a state licence covering that scope.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Santa Clarita's pool drainage form allows the sewer only through a 3-inch P-trap installed under a city permit, never between 5 and 9 am on weekdays and never into a septic system. Water sent to the street gutter needs notice to Environmental Services at 661-286-4098 and must be dechlorinated, debrominated and pH neutral with no algaecides, and saltwater pools may not drain to the gutter or storm drain at all. The city's pool guidance limits the flow to one outlet no wider than 2 inches.

Sellers in Santa Clarita should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common Santa Clarita questions

Does a Santa Clarita pool filled in place stay on record?
Yes. The pool abandonment bulletin (revised January 2020) says the property will be permanently recorded as having an abandoned structure.

The pool may have to be removed completely if a structure is proposed on or near it.

How many holes does Santa Clarita require?
At least two 24-inch holes, with the bottom broken up for drainage.

Does Santa Clarita need a compaction report?
