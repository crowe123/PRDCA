Fremont Pool Removal Permit Rules

Fremont's fee schedule (amended January 2026) lists residential swimming pool demolition and removal as an express permit at a flat $266, under a heading for permits with no plan review and inspection only. Extra inspections and reinspections cost $133 each. The Building and Safety FAQ says a building permit is needed before demolishing any structure, and the city publishes no technical rules for the holes, the wall or the fill.

The Community Development Department, Building and Safety is the office to call before any work starts. Fremont is one of 11 cities among the 32 in this directory that publish nothing specific to pools, so the department's answer on the phone decides the permit type, the fee and the fill standard.

Air district notice in Fremont

The Bay Area Air Quality Management District asks for notice at least 10 business days before every demolition, regardless of asbestos content, with a 72-hour option for residential buildings of four units or fewer. Its rule defines demolition as removing load-supporting members of a building or facility and does not mention pools, so the district confirms whether a pool-only job needs the notice.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Fremont

With no published fill rules and no plan review, the inspection is where Fremont judges the method. An owner planning to build over the area later should settle the compaction standard with Building and Safety before the work starts, since the express permit leaves no plan on file that sets it.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

Hauling the debris in Fremont

Fremont's construction and demolition page lists all demolition among the projects with recycling requirements: a Waste Handling Plan goes into Fremont Waste Tracking before the permit is issued, and a Debris Disposal and Diversion Report closes the job. The city's plan form requires 100% of asphalt, concrete and soil recycled, with a penalty of $1,000 for each ton that is not. Republic Services is the only approved debris box hauler at construction sites, though separate loads of clean soil, asphalt or concrete are exempt when each is no more than 10% garbage, and contractors may self-haul to an approved facility.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Union Sanitary District, which serves Fremont, warns that pool water drained to a storm drain flows to creeks and the San Francisco Bay. The Clean Water Program guidance it links sends pool water to a sanitary sewer cleanout and prohibits draining water with residual chlorine or copper algaecide to a storm drain, and the district's number for sewer approval is (510) 477-7500.

Sellers in Fremont should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common Fremont questions

What does a Fremont pool removal permit cost?

$266 flat for a residential pool demolition or removal, on the schedule amended in January 2026.

Does a Fremont pool removal need plans?

No. The city lists it as an express permit with no plan review and inspection only.

What happens to the concrete from a Fremont pool?

It must be 100% recycled, with a penalty of $1,000 a ton for any that is not.

Who can supply a debris box in Fremont?

Republic Services, though separate loads of clean concrete, asphalt or soil are exempt.

Where can Fremont pool water be drained?

To a sanitary sewer cleanout with Union Sanitary District approval; chlorinated water may not go to the storm drain.
