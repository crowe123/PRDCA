A contractor's licence classes matter on a pool job. Ontario permits are generally issued to holders of the A (general engineering), C-12 (earthwork and paving), C-21 (building moving and demolition) or C-53 (swimming pool) classes described in the state licence board's classification guide, and the accepted classes differ from city to city. Industry Bulletin 24-07 confirms that the $1,000 unlicensed-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit, so a pool removal always needs a licensed contractor or an owner-builder permit.

Ontario Pool Removal Permit Rules

Ontario's Building Department issues a building permit for pool and spa demolition through its Citizen Portal, with plan review. Information Bulletin 111, effective 16 October 2024, requires the entire shell removed along with the above-ground equipment, gas lines capped and the electrical subpanel and conductors removed. The fill is self-compacting material such as sand, or clean dirt compacted to at least 90%, shown on a to-scale site plan. Fees follow the city's valuation-based building permit table.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Ontario

Ontario allows only one route: the whole shell comes out. Abandonment in place is not addressed in the bulletin, so a partial fill-in is not an option without the Building Department's approval. Sand fill, which compacts itself, can avoid compaction testing, a cost worth comparing against clean dirt.

A partial fill-in breaks drainage holes through the floor, removes the top of the wall and fills the shell with soil. The method costs less and finishes faster, usually in one to four days, but most cities limit the area to landscaping afterwards. A full removal hauls away every piece of the shell and replaces it with fill compacted in layers to 90%, which takes three to seven days and is the route for anyone planning a structure over the old pool.

Published prices for Inland Empire

No contractor in this part of California publishes local prices, so the statewide figures apply. Northern California firms such as Walberg, Inc. quote $3,500 to $12,000 for a partial fill-in and $8,000 to $30,000 for a full removal, and Los Angeles firms start at $10,500 and $17,500. National guides such as HomeAdvisor run lower and work best as a floor.

Four things move a quote more than anything else: equipment access (about $2,000 more where the side yard is under 6 ft wide), deck removal ($500 to $1,700), fill dirt (80 to 150 cubic yards for a full removal) and the engineer's compaction report ($1,500 to $4,000). The California pool removal cost guide sets out every published price by region and pool type.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The San Diego pools and spas fact sheet and LA Stormwater both allow the storm drain only for dechlorinated water within a set pH range, and Ontario contractors generally follow the same practice. The Building Department confirms the local rule.

Removing a pool can lower the assessed value of a Ontario property. The Board of Equalization's letter 86/09 treats removal of an improvement as a reason to reduce assessed value, but the reduction is tied to the pool's own assessed value, so an older pool may bring only a small change. Sellers should also expect the question on the state's seller disclosure form, which asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

What fill does Ontario accept?
Self-compacting fill such as sand, or clean dirt compacted to at least 90%.
