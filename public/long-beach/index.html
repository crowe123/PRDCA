A contractor's licence classes matter on a pool job. Long Beach permits are generally issued to holders of the A (general engineering), C-12 (earthwork and paving), C-21 (building moving and demolition) or C-53 (swimming pool) classes described in the state licence board's classification guide, and the accepted classes differ from city to city. Industry Bulletin 24-07 confirms that the $1,000 unlicensed-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit, so a pool removal always needs a licensed contractor or an owner-builder permit.

Long Beach Pool Removal Permit Rules

Long Beach's Building and Safety Bureau handles pool removal on a grading permit, and Information Bulletin IB-008 sets the rules. Leaving the shell in place needs two 24-inch holes through the bottom and a recorded covenant against the property, and the area is limited to landscaping. The bulletin publishes no pool fee; the grading fee follows the scope of work.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Long Beach

The recorded covenant is the main difference between the two Long Beach routes. A partial fill-in stays on the property record and limits the area to landscaping, which buyers and lenders will see. A full removal with compacted fill avoids the covenant and keeps the area open for a later structure.

A partial fill-in breaks drainage holes through the floor, removes the top of the wall and fills the shell with soil. The method costs less and finishes faster, usually in one to four days, but most cities limit the area to landscaping afterwards. A full removal hauls away every piece of the shell and replaces it with fill compacted in layers to 90%, which takes three to seven days and is the route for anyone planning a structure over the old pool.

Published prices for Los Angeles County

Los Angeles Pool Demolition Contractors publishes prices from $10,500 for a partial fill-in and from $17,500 for a full removal. Angi's Los Angeles cost page reports a wider spread of $2,850 to $11,350 for a partial and $4,550 to $18,200 for a full removal, with an average of $6,816. Contractor quotes in Los Angeles County sit nearer the top of the Angi range than its average.

Four things move a quote more than anything else: equipment access (about $2,000 more where the side yard is under 6 ft wide), deck removal ($500 to $1,700), fill dirt (80 to 150 cubic yards for a full removal) and the engineer's compaction report ($1,500 to $4,000). The California pool removal cost guide sets out every published price by region and pool type.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The San Diego pools and spas fact sheet and LA Stormwater both allow the storm drain only for dechlorinated water within a set pH range, and Long Beach contractors generally follow the same practice. The Community Development Department, Building and Safety Bureau confirms the local rule.

Removing a pool can lower the assessed value of a Long Beach property. The Board of Equalization's letter 86/09 treats removal of an improvement as a reason to reduce assessed value, but the reduction is tied to the pool's own assessed value, so an older pool may bring only a small change. Sellers should also expect the question on the state's seller disclosure form, which asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common Long Beach questions

Does Long Beach record a partial pool fill-in?
Yes. Leaving the shell needs a recorded covenant on the property and two 24-inch holes in the bottom.

Which permit covers pool removal in Long Beach?
A grading permit, under Information Bulletin IB-008.

Can a Long Beach filled pool area be built on?
Not after a partial fill-in, which is limited to landscaping; full removal with compacted fill is needed.
