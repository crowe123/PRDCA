Long Beach Pool Removal Permit Rules

Long Beach's Information Bulletin IB-008 (revised January 2025) sets out two methods, and each needs a grading permit. The city's building fee schedule effective 1 October 2026 prices grading permits by volume: $104 for 50 cubic yards or less, $208 for 51 to 100, and $209 plus $64 for each further 100 cubic yards up to 1,000, with plan review at 75% of the permit fee. Under both methods the gas and plumbing lines are capped and the electrical equipment removed.

The Long Beach covenant

For a shell left in place, the owner signs the city's Covenant and Agreement for maintenance of uncertified fill, agreeing to use the area only as open space and not for any structural support. The bureau approves it, it is recorded with the Los Angeles County Recorder, and a certified recorded copy is required before the grading permit is issued. The covenant runs with the land, binds future owners and stays in force until the Building Official releases it on request, for a fee.

Each method has two inspections:

  1. A bottom inspection: of the drainage holes before any fill on a shell left in place, or of the excavation before fill on a structural fill.
  2. A final inspection once the fill is complete.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Long Beach

A structural fill removes the shell from the site and compacts the fill to 90% relative compaction under ASTM D1557, tested by an agency the bureau approves, with a soils report when required; no foundation goes in until the compaction report is approved. Leaving the shell in place is allowed only where the fill will not support buildings, on ground sloping 10% or less. It needs two drainage holes at least 24 inches across, no more than 8 ft apart with one at the deepest point, and clean soil or gravel moistened and tamped, and the area is used for landscaping only.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

Hauling the debris in Long Beach

Long Beach's construction and demolition recycling program requires at least 65% of waste diverted and a management plan for all demolition projects of any valuation. Debris must be self-hauled or carried by a city-permitted hauler, and using an unlisted hauler can forfeit the deposit. Receipts and a final compliance report are due within 90 days of the final inspection. The October 2026 fee schedule sets the residential administrative fee at $238 and the deposit at 3% of valuation, with a $1,712 minimum. The program does not say whether a pool backfill counts as a demolition project, so the bureau confirms it.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The city's Health Department pool guidance allows dechlorinated pool water to the storm drain only at 0.1 mg/L of chlorine or less, a pH of 6.5 to 8.5, with no colour, dirt, algae, filter media or acid waste. An older Public Works pamphlet sends pool water to the sanitary sewer under a permit instead, so Public Works Stormwater at (562) 570-6383 confirms the current route.

Sellers in Long Beach should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common Long Beach questions

Does a Long Beach pool removal need a grading permit?

Yes, whether the shell stays or is removed.

What does Long Beach require to leave a shell in place?

Two drainage holes at least 24 inches across, no more than 8 ft apart, a recorded covenant and landscaping use only.

Is the Long Beach covenant permanent?

It runs with the land and binds future owners until the Building Official releases it.

What does a Long Beach grading permit cost?

$104 for up to 50 cubic yards or $208 for 51 to 100, plus plan review at 75%, on the October 2026 schedule.

Can a Long Beach pool area be built on later?

Only after a structural fill: shell removed and fill compacted to 90% with an approved compaction report.
