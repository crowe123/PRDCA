A contractor's licence classes matter on a pool job. Palmdale permits are generally issued to holders of the A (general engineering), C-12 (earthwork and paving), C-21 (building moving and demolition) or C-53 (swimming pool) classes described in the state licence board's classification guide, and the accepted classes differ from city to city. Industry Bulletin 24-07 confirms that the $1,000 unlicensed-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit, so a pool removal always needs a licensed contractor or an owner-builder permit.

Palmdale Pool Removal Permit Rules

Palmdale publishes no pool-specific rule. The city's Building and Safety Development Guide, updated January 2026, requires a demolition permit for each structure demolished in part or whole, and the municipal code defines demolition to include filling the excavation it leaves. Demolition permits need Antelope Valley Air Quality Management District approval, a hazardous materials evaluation and a construction waste plan with deposit. The 2023 fee resolution shows the demolition inspection as both $200 and $207.50.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Palmdale

Without a published pool rule, the Building and Safety Division decides whether a partial fill-in is accepted in Palmdale. Because the code counts the fill as part of the demolition, the inspector will expect a compaction standard either way; full removal with certified compaction is the route for any later structure.

A partial fill-in breaks drainage holes through the floor, removes the top of the wall and fills the shell with soil. The method costs less and finishes faster, usually in one to four days, but most cities limit the area to landscaping afterwards. A full removal hauls away every piece of the shell and replaces it with fill compacted in layers to 90%, which takes three to seven days and is the route for anyone planning a structure over the old pool.

Published prices for Los Angeles County

No contractor in this part of California publishes local prices, so the statewide figures apply. Northern California firms such as Walberg, Inc. quote $3,500 to $12,000 for a partial fill-in and $8,000 to $30,000 for a full removal, and Los Angeles firms start at $10,500 and $17,500. National guides such as HomeAdvisor run lower and work best as a floor.

Four things move a quote more than anything else: equipment access (about $2,000 more where the side yard is under 6 ft wide), deck removal ($500 to $1,700), fill dirt (80 to 150 cubic yards for a full removal) and the engineer's compaction report ($1,500 to $4,000). The California pool removal cost guide sets out every published price by region and pool type.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The San Diego pools and spas fact sheet and LA Stormwater both allow the storm drain only for dechlorinated water within a set pH range, and Palmdale contractors generally follow the same practice. The Building and Safety Division confirms the local rule.

Removing a pool can lower the assessed value of a Palmdale property. The Board of Equalization's letter 86/09 treats removal of an improvement as a reason to reduce assessed value, but the reduction is tied to the pool's own assessed value, so an older pool may bring only a small change. Sellers should also expect the question on the state's seller disclosure form, which asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.
