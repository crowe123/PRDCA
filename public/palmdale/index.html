Palmdale Pool Removal Permit Rules

Palmdale publishes no pool-specific rule. The city's Building and Safety Development Guide, updated January 2026, requires a demolition permit for each structure demolished in part or whole, and Palmdale Municipal Code section 8.04.200 defines demolition to include filling the excavations it exposes. Demolition permits need approval from outside agencies including the Antelope Valley Air Quality Management District, uploaded to Accela, and a hazardous materials evaluation. The city's FY 2026 fee schedule, effective July 2025, sets the demolition inspection, including sewage system termination, at $222.

The Building and Safety Division is the office to call before any work starts. Palmdale is one of 10 cities among the 32 in this directory that publish nothing specific to pools, so the department's answer on the phone decides the permit type, the fee and the fill standard.

Air district rules in Palmdale

The Antelope Valley Air Quality Management District's Rule 1403 requires notification at least 10 working days before any demolition of a facility, and defines demolition as taking out a load-supporting structural member. The rule does not mention pools, so the district confirms whether a pool-only job counts. Its Rule 403 on dust applies to every site: no dust visible beyond the property line, and track-out cleaned up at the end of each workday.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Palmdale

Without a pool rule, the grading code is the closest standard: it calls for fill compacted to 90% of maximum density under ASTM D1557, in lifts of 12 inches or less. The code does not tie that standard to pools, so the Building and Safety Division confirms whether a partial fill-in is accepted and what compaction it expects.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

Hauling the debris in Palmdale

Palmdale's construction and demolition chapter requires at least 65% of debris tonnage diverted, a waste management plan for every permitted demolition, and a deposit of 2% of the estimated construction cost, between $1,000 and $100,000, plus a $77.25 application fee. Only the city's franchised collector, WM, may haul the waste, unless a contractor carries its own debris in a fixed-body truck, and the city's plan form names Lancaster WM as the recycling facility. Deposit refunds need landfill and recycling receipts before the final inspection is scheduled.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Palmdale's sewer code bars stormwater and surface water from the public sewer, and the city's stormwater management plan lists dechlorinated swimming pool water among allowed discharges. The city publishes no current pool-draining guidance, so the Building and Safety Division confirms the route.

Sellers in Palmdale should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common Palmdale questions

Does Palmdale count the fill as part of the demolition?
Yes. Municipal Code section 8.04.200 defines demolition to include filling the excavations it exposes.

What does a Palmdale demolition permit cost?
$222 for the demolition inspection on the FY 2026 schedule, plus the debris plan fee and deposit.

How much is the Palmdale debris deposit?
2% of the estimated construction cost, at least $1,000 and at most $100,000.

Who hauls pool debris in Palmdale?
WM, the city's franchised collector, to the Lancaster WM facility; contractors may carry their own debris in fixed-body trucks.
