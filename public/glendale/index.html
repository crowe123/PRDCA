Glendale Pool Removal Permit Rules

Glendale publishes no pool-specific permit. The city's permit application guidance page sends demolition through the "Apply for Building Demo" link in the GlendalePermits portal, with a site plan, a renovation and demolition acknowledgement form for the South Coast air district's asbestos rule, and a lead abatement acknowledgement. The same page says no sewer-cap plumbing permit is needed when the demolition is a swimming pool. No fee line for pool demolition was found.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Glendale

Glendale's guidance says pool backfill projects require Design Review from Planning, and the backfill must be maintained as landscaping under the State Model Water Efficient Landscape Ordinance, adopted in Glendale Municipal Code 30.31. An owner who wants the area for something other than landscaping must show the project meets every development standard of the zone. The city publishes no rules on holes, shell removal or compaction, so Building and Safety confirms what it expects.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

Hauling the debris in Glendale

Glendale's construction and demolition recycling ordinance refunds its deposit when a project diverts 65% of its estimated debris, with original facility tags due within six months. The deposit is 6% of valuation for demolition, up to $30,000, and is waived when it would be $200 or less. Whether a pool removal falls under the ordinance is not stated, so Building and Safety confirms it. The city keeps a list of permitted haulers for construction debris and roll-off bins, including Athens Services and Waste Management.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Glendale's stormwater rules ban discharges to the storm drain except a few allowed conditionally, such as pool dewatering, and the regional permit's conditional exemption table requires dechlorinated water at no more than 0.1 mg/L of chlorine, a pH of 6.5 to 8.5, no copper algaecides and 72 hours' notice for 100,000 gallons or more. Filter backwash needs a separate permit, and the city's fee schedule lists fines of $100 to $500 for draining a pool onto another property or into the storm drain against the rules.

Sellers in Glendale should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common Glendale questions

Does a Glendale pool backfill need Design Review?
Yes. The city's permit guidance says pool backfill projects require Design Review from Planning.

What can a Glendale backfilled pool area be used for?
Landscaping under the water efficient landscape rules, or another use that meets every zoning development standard.

Does a Glendale pool demolition need a sewer-cap permit?
No. The guidance exempts swimming pools from the sewer-cap plumbing permit.

Can Glendale pool water go to the storm drain?
Only dechlorinated to 0.1 mg/L or less at pH 6.5 to 8.5, with no copper algaecides.
