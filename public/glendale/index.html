A contractor's licence classes matter on a pool job. Glendale permits are generally issued to holders of the A (general engineering), C-12 (earthwork and paving), C-21 (building moving and demolition) or C-53 (swimming pool) classes described in the state licence board's classification guide, and the accepted classes differ from city to city. Industry Bulletin 24-07 confirms that the $1,000 unlicensed-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit, so a pool removal always needs a licensed contractor or an owner-builder permit.

Glendale Pool Removal Permit Rules

Glendale publishes no pool-specific permit. The city's permit application guidance page says demolition permits are applied for as "Building Demo" in the GlendalePermits portal, with a site plan, a renovation-demolition acknowledgement form and a lead abatement acknowledgement. Pool backfill projects also need Design Review from Planning, and the backfilled area must stay as landscaping under the city's water efficient landscape rules or meet all zoning development standards. Partial fill-in or full removal in Glendale

Glendale's Design Review step applies to any pool backfill, so the Planning review sits on the schedule whichever method is chosen. Glendale publishes no rules on holes, shell removal or compaction, so the Building and Safety Section confirms what it expects. A backfilled area kept as landscaping must meet the water efficient landscape rules.

A partial fill-in breaks drainage holes through the floor, removes the top of the wall and fills the shell with soil. The method costs less and finishes faster, usually in one to four days, but most cities limit the area to landscaping afterwards. A full removal hauls away every piece of the shell and replaces it with fill compacted in layers to 90%, which takes three to seven days and is the route for anyone planning a structure over the old pool.

Published prices for Los Angeles County

Los Angeles Pool Demolition Contractors publishes prices from $10,500 for a partial fill-in and from $17,500 for a full removal. Angi's Los Angeles cost page reports a wider spread of $2,850 to $11,350 for a partial and $4,550 to $18,200 for a full removal, with an average of $6,816. Contractor quotes in Los Angeles County sit nearer the top of the Angi range than its average.

Four things move a quote more than anything else: equipment access (about $2,000 more where the side yard is under 6 ft wide), deck removal ($500 to $1,700), fill dirt (80 to 150 cubic yards for a full removal) and the engineer's compaction report ($1,500 to $4,000). The California pool removal cost guide sets out every published price by region and pool type.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The San Diego pools and spas fact sheet and LA Stormwater both allow the storm drain only for dechlorinated water within a set pH range, and Glendale contractors generally follow the same practice. The Community Development Department, Building and Safety Section confirms the local rule.

Removing a pool can lower the assessed value of a Glendale property. The Board of Equalization's letter 86/09 treats removal of an improvement as a reason to reduce assessed value, but the reduction is tied to the pool's own assessed value, so an older pool may bring only a small change. Sellers should also expect the question on the state's seller disclosure form, which asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.
