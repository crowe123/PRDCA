A contractor's licence classes matter on a pool job. Fresno permits are generally issued to holders of the A (general engineering), C-12 (earthwork and paving), C-21 (building moving and demolition) or C-53 (swimming pool) classes described in the state licence board's classification guide, and the accepted classes differ from city to city. Industry Bulletin 24-07 confirms that the $1,000 unlicensed-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit, so a pool removal always needs a licensed contractor or an owner-builder permit.

Fresno Pool Removal Permit Rules

Fresno issues a Swimming Pool Abandonment Permit through its Planning and Development Department, set out in Bulletin 13-02. The bulletin's fee of $321 dates from 2013 and should be confirmed with the city before a job is priced. Fresno's rule is strict: the entire bottom must be broken out, and all backfill must be engineered to 90% compaction. Owner-builders may apply, and the bulletin names the C-12 and A licence classes.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Fresno

Fresno's abandonment rules leave less difference between the two routes than most cities. Breaking out the whole bottom and compacting every layer to 90% means even a partial job carries engineering and fill costs close to a full removal. Owners who expect to build later usually choose full removal for the cleaner record.

A partial fill-in breaks drainage holes through the floor, removes the top of the wall and fills the shell with soil. The method costs less and finishes faster, usually in one to four days, but most cities limit the area to landscaping afterwards. A full removal hauls away every piece of the shell and replaces it with fill compacted in layers to 90%, which takes three to seven days and is the route for anyone planning a structure over the old pool.

Published prices for Sacramento and the Central Valley

No contractor in this part of California publishes local prices, so the statewide figures apply. Northern California firms such as Walberg, Inc. quote $3,500 to $12,000 for a partial fill-in and $8,000 to $30,000 for a full removal, and Los Angeles firms start at $10,500 and $17,500. National guides such as HomeAdvisor run lower and work best as a floor.

Four things move a quote more than anything else: equipment access (about $2,000 more where the side yard is under 6 ft wide), deck removal ($500 to $1,700), fill dirt (80 to 150 cubic yards for a full removal) and the engineer's compaction report ($1,500 to $4,000). The California pool removal cost guide sets out every published price by region and pool type.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The San Diego pools and spas fact sheet and LA Stormwater both allow the storm drain only for dechlorinated water within a set pH range, and Fresno contractors generally follow the same practice. The Planning and Development Department confirms the local rule.

Removing a pool can lower the assessed value of a Fresno property. The Board of Equalization's letter 86/09 treats removal of an improvement as a reason to reduce assessed value, but the reduction is tied to the pool's own assessed value, so an older pool may bring only a small change. Sellers should also expect the question on the state's seller disclosure form, which asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common Fresno questions

What is the Fresno pool abandonment fee?
$321 in Bulletin 13-02, a 2013 figure that needs a recheck with the city before budgeting.

Does Fresno require the pool bottom removed?
Yes. The entire bottom must be broken out and all backfill engineered to 90% compaction.

Can a Fresno homeowner pull the abandonment permit?
Yes. Fresno accepts owner-builders, and contractors need a C-12 or A licence.
