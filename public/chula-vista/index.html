A contractor's licence classes matter on a pool job. Chula Vista permits are generally issued to holders of the A (general engineering), C-12 (earthwork and paving), C-21 (building moving and demolition) or C-53 (swimming pool) classes described in the state licence board's classification guide, and the accepted classes differ from city to city. Industry Bulletin 24-07 confirms that the $1,000 unlicensed-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit, so a pool removal always needs a licensed contractor or an owner-builder permit.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Chula Vista

Chula Vista publishes no rule on what may stay in the ground, so the Development Services Department confirms whether a partial fill-in is accepted. The San Diego bulletin, which most contractors working both cities follow, requires a soils compaction report for pools deeper than five feet.

A partial fill-in breaks drainage holes through the floor, removes the top of the wall and fills the shell with soil. The method costs less and finishes faster, usually in one to four days, but most cities limit the area to landscaping afterwards. A full removal hauls away every piece of the shell and replaces it with fill compacted in layers to 90%, which takes three to seven days and is the route for anyone planning a structure over the old pool.

Published prices for San Diego County

San Diego's two published price lists disagree by a factor of three or more. Demo Diego quotes $5,000 to $9,000 for a partial fill-in and $9,000 to $15,000 for a full removal, while Villa Demolition quotes $12,000 to $30,000 and $45,000 to $60,000. A written quote from more than one contractor is the only reliable way to place a pool inside that spread.

Four things move a quote more than anything else: equipment access (about $2,000 more where the side yard is under 6 ft wide), deck removal ($500 to $1,700), fill dirt (80 to 150 cubic yards for a full removal) and the engineer's compaction report ($1,500 to $4,000). The California pool removal cost guide sets out every published price by region and pool type.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The San Diego pools and spas fact sheet and LA Stormwater both allow the storm drain only for dechlorinated water within a set pH range, and Chula Vista contractors generally follow the same practice. The Development Services Department confirms the local rule.

Removing a pool can lower the assessed value of a Chula Vista property. The Board of Equalization's letter 86/09 treats removal of an improvement as a reason to reduce assessed value, but the reduction is tied to the pool's own assessed value, so an older pool may bring only a small change. Sellers should also expect the question on the state's seller disclosure form, which asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.
