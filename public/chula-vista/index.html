Chula Vista Pool Removal Permit Rules

Chula Vista's Development Services Department requires a demolition permit whenever a building or structure is demolished. The city's demolition permit page asks for a New Construction Worksheet, the San Diego regional hazardous materials questionnaire and utility disconnect notices for anything connected to a utility. The city's Fee Bulletin 10-400, from the September 2024 master fee schedule, has a line for swimming pool, spa and hot tub demolition at $123 intake and $281 inspection, $404 in total, and all fees are paid before the permit is issued.

Hauling the debris in Chula Vista

Chula Vista's construction and demolition rules require 100% of inert materials such as concrete, and at least 65% of everything else, recycled. Environmental Services must approve a Waste Management Report before a demolition permit is issued, which takes up to 10 business days, and diversion records are due within 30 days after a demolition-only job finishes. Republic Services is the city's franchise hauler and rents containers at 619-421-9400. The city also takes a refundable performance deposit of 1.5% of valuation on most larger projects, so Environmental Services confirms whether a pool job needs one.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Chula Vista's pool maintenance guidance sends it to the sanitary sewer, or slowly onto landscaping with no runoff, and allows the storm drain only when chlorine tests below one part per million, the pH is 7 to 8 and the water is free of colour, algae and debris. Large volumes to the sewer need a call to Wastewater Engineering at (619) 691-5021 first, and saltwater pool water may not go to the storm drain.
