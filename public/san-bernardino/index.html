A contractor's licence classes matter on a pool job. San Bernardino permits are generally issued to holders of the A (general engineering), C-12 (earthwork and paving), C-21 (building moving and demolition) or C-53 (swimming pool) classes described in the state licence board's classification guide, and the accepted classes differ from city to city. Industry Bulletin 24-07 confirms that the $1,000 unlicensed-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit, so a pool removal always needs a licensed contractor or an owner-builder permit.

San Bernardino Pool Removal Permit Rules

San Bernardino treats pool removal as on-site grading, so the fee is based on the cubic yards of fill. The city's pool demolition handout (2017) requires 12-inch holes every 6 ft across the bottom and the top two feet of wall cut down when the shell stays.

Partial fill-in or full removal in San Bernardino

San Bernardino's fill-based fee means a full removal, which takes 80 to 150 cubic yards of fill, costs more in permit fees as well as dirt. A partial fill-in uses less fill and suits a yard staying as landscape.

A partial fill-in breaks drainage holes through the floor, removes the top of the wall and fills the shell with soil. The method costs less and finishes faster, usually in one to four days, but most cities limit the area to landscaping afterwards. A full removal hauls away every piece of the shell and replaces it with fill compacted in layers to 90%, which takes three to seven days and is the route for anyone planning a structure over the old pool.

Published prices for Inland Empire

No contractor in this part of California publishes local prices, so the statewide figures apply. Northern California firms such as Walberg, Inc. quote $3,500 to $12,000 for a partial fill-in and $8,000 to $30,000 for a full removal, and Los Angeles firms start at $10,500 and $17,500. National guides such as HomeAdvisor run lower and work best as a floor.

Four things move a quote more than anything else: equipment access (about $2,000 more where the side yard is under 6 ft wide), deck removal ($500 to $1,700), fill dirt (80 to 150 cubic yards for a full removal) and the engineer's compaction report ($1,500 to $4,000). The California pool removal cost guide sets out every published price by region and pool type.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The San Diego pools and spas fact sheet and LA Stormwater both allow the storm drain only for dechlorinated water within a set pH range, and San Bernardino contractors generally follow the same practice. The Community and Economic Development Department is the office to ask about the local rule.

Removing a pool can lower the assessed value of a San Bernardino property. The Board of Equalization's letter 86/09 treats removal of an improvement as a reason to reduce assessed value, but the reduction is tied to the pool's own assessed value, so an older pool may bring only a small change. Sellers should also expect the question on the state's seller disclosure form, which asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.
