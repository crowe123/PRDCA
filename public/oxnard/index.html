Oxnard Pool Removal Permit Rules

Oxnard publishes no pool demolition or abandonment handout, and demolition needs a permit from the Building and Engineering Division of the Community Development Department. Air district notice in Oxnard

The Ventura County Air Pollution Control District says a building or home must be surveyed for asbestos to obtain a demolition permit. Its notification form asks for notice and a 10 working day wait on subject demolitions even without asbestos, while residential buildings of four units or fewer skip the wait and fee. The district does not mention pools, so it confirms whether a pool-only job counts.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Oxnard

No city document sets drainage holes, shell depth or compaction rules for pools, so the Building and Engineering Division decides whether a partial fill-in is accepted. Oxnard's grading code exempts isolated, self-contained earthwork of up to 150 cubic yards that does not affect neighbours, and requires a soils engineering report with any grading permit application, so a full removal with a large fill volume may bring in the grading rules.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

Hauling the debris in Oxnard

Oxnard's construction and demolition waste plan requires 65% of the debris recycled or salvaged, approved by the Environmental Resources Division before work starts, with a report and receipts before final inspection. The city rents its own roll-off bins, including a 10-yard bin for dirt and concrete only, and charges $139.92 a ton for construction debris at the Del Norte Regional Recycling and Transfer Station, open Monday to Saturday.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Oxnard's stormwater code allows dechlorinated pool water at no more than 0.1 mg/L of chlorine or bromine, with no chemicals or algaecides and controlled volume and velocity, and bans filter backwash from the storm drain. The city's sewer code bars swimming pool drainage from the sewer unless the city manager specifically authorizes it.

Sellers in Oxnard should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.
