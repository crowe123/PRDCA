Santa Ana Pool Removal Permit Rules

Santa Ana's Planning and Building Agency requires three permits to abandon a pool: a building permit with a site plan, an electrical permit and a plumbing permit, plus approvals from the Planning Department and from Public Works for construction runoff. The city's 2026-27 fee schedule bills demolition plan check at $294.06 an hour and inspection at $212.11 an hour, with an electrical demolition fee of $64.15 and a 26% stormwater surcharge on building and plumbing permits.

Inspections and paperwork on a Santa Ana job

BLD-16 sets the inspection order, booked at (714) 667-2738 or online:

  1. Electrical inspection, with every electrical connection terminated or disconnected.
  2. Plumbing inspection, with every water supply and drain connection capped.
  3. Building inspection of the holes, which gives the go-ahead to backfill once electrical and plumbing have passed.
  4. Final inspection, where any compaction report goes to the inspector and is filed with the finaled permit.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Santa Ana

Handout BLD-16 (revised March 2025) describes abandoning the pool in place. Crews jack hammer five holes, each 3 ft by 3 ft, four in the deep end and one in the shallow end, remove the bond beam around the top, typically 12 to 18 inches deep, and take out any built-in spa walls. The shell is filled with pea gravel up to the bond beam and finished with clean soil, or filled with soil compacted to 90% of the laboratory standard, with a compaction report at final inspection. An addition over the old pool needs a compaction report from a professional engineer.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Santa Ana belongs to H2OC, the stormwater program shared by all 34 Orange County cities, whose pool drainage brochure asks for pool water sent to the sanitary sewer when possible. The storm drain is allowed only for dechlorinated water with no more than 0.1 mg/L of chlorine, a pH of 6.5 to 8.5 and no filter media or acid waste, and saltwater pools may not use it. Santa Ana's storm drains carry water untreated to the ocean.

Common Santa Ana questions

What permits does a Santa Ana pool removal need?

Building, electrical and plumbing permits, plus Planning and Public Works approval.

How many holes does Santa Ana require?

Five holes, each 3 ft by 3 ft: four in the deep end and one in the shallow end.

Can a Santa Ana homeowner build over a filled pool?

Yes, with a compaction report from a professional engineer, as Handout BLD-16 (revised March 2025) requires for an addition.

What does a Santa Ana pool removal permit cost?

Demolition plan check is $294.06 an hour and inspection $212.11 an hour on the 2026-27 schedule, plus electrical and plumbing fees.

Where can Santa Ana pool water be drained?

To the sanitary sewer when possible; the storm drain only below 0.1 mg/L chlorine at pH 6.5 to 8.5.
