Oakland Pool Removal Permit Rules

Oakland publishes no rule specific to pool removal, and its permit pages do not say which permit covers one, so Planning & Building on Frank H. Ogawa Plaza ((510) 238-3891) confirms the permit type and fee. The city's grading permits page sets the triggers that can apply to the fill: a grading permit is needed for more than 50 cubic yards of fill where a slope exceeds 20% or the fill is more than 5 ft high, for more than 500 cubic yards of cut and fill combined, for any land disturbance on ground steeper than 20%, and wherever the city has information about land stability problems. Grading applications go through the Online Permit Center with an Initial Statement of the Engineer for Grading.

Air district notice in Oakland

Oakland's demolition permit page requires an asbestos demolition acknowledgment letter and job number from the Bay Area Air Quality Management District before a building demolition permit is issued. The district's rule defines demolition as removing load-supporting members of a building or facility and does not mention pools, so the district or Planning & Building confirms whether a pool-only job needs the notice.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Oakland

Oakland's calendar shapes the job more than the method. The city's wet weather grading moratorium prohibits disturbing soil from 15 October to 15 April, with exceptions only for emergency stabilization or an alternate methods request backed by a geotechnical engineer's letter, shoring plans and a schedule. A pool on a lot steeper than 20% falls inside the grading permit rules, which bring in the grading engineer's statement whichever method is used. On flatter ground, Planning & Building confirms whether a partial fill-in is accepted.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

What a Oakland job costs

No Oakland contractor publishes prices, but Bay Area firms do: Souza & Son's publishes $4,500 to $10,000 for a partial fill-in and $8,000 to $22,000 for a full removal, and Walberg, Inc. gives $5,000 to $12,000 and $12,000 to $30,000 for Northern California. On a hillside lot, the grading engineer's statement and any grading permit add to those figures.

The California pool removal cost guide covers what moves a quote, from side-yard access to fill dirt and the compaction report.

Hauling the debris in Oakland

Oakland's construction and demolition recycling rules apply to all city building permits: debris goes to facilities that recover 100% of inert material such as concrete, asphalt and soil, and 65% of everything else, with a Waste Reduction and Recycling Plan filed online through Green Halo before the permit and a summary report at the end. Anyone hauling the debris needs a non-exclusive franchise from the city under Chapter 15.34 of the municipal code. Property owners are exempt, and so are contractors hauling their own work's debris in their own fixed-body trucks, but that exemption does not cover demolition contractors.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Oakland's temporary discharge permit page says only rainwater should enter the city's storm drains, and other water needs a temporary storm drain or sanitary sewer discharge permit. The Alameda County Clean Water Program guidance for pools sends pool water to a sanitary sewer cleanout, prohibits draining water with residual chlorine or copper algaecide to a storm drain, and names EBMUD at (510) 287-1651 for sewer approval in Oakland.

Sellers in Oakland should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common Oakland questions

Does Oakland publish pool removal rules?

No. Planning & Building at (510) 238-3891 confirms the permit type and fee.

When can a pool be removed in Oakland?

Between 15 April and 15 October. The wet weather moratorium bars soil disturbance the rest of the year.

Does an Oakland pool removal need a grading permit?

It can. More than 50 cubic yards of fill on a slope over 20% or over 5 ft high, more than 500 cubic yards in total, or work on ground steeper than 20% needs one.

Where can Oakland pool water be drained?

To a sanitary sewer cleanout with EBMUD's approval; chlorinated water may not go to the storm drain.

Who can haul pool debris in Oakland?

A city-franchised hauler, the property owner, or a contractor hauling its own work in its own trucks; demolition contractors need the franchise.
