Garden Grove Pool Removal Permit Rules

Garden Grove requires a demolition permit for every pool and spa demolition, obtained through the Development Access portal, and Information Bulletin 26-012 (March 2026) sets the method. The city's building fee schedule has no demolition line; it lists $81 for pool electrical work and $81 for pool plumbing, with a $64 issuance fee. An older 2020 pool demolition policy is still online and is stricter: it cut the walls 24 inches below grade and put a soils engineer on every job, so owners should follow the 2026 bulletin.

Inspections on a Garden Grove job

IB 26-012 lists the inspections:

  1. Electrical wiring, conduit and raceways disconnected and removed.
  2. Gas lines disconnected and capped at the meter and the equipment.
  3. The drainage openings, before backfill.
  4. The rock and sand backfill, by city inspection or geotechnical observation.
  5. A final grading inspection, with the grade set to prevent ponding.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Garden Grove

The bulletin leaves the shell with at least two drainage openings of at least 12 inches, one at the lowest point, and the bottom passes inspection before any backfill. At least 12 inches of No. 4 rock goes in, deck and coping rubble no larger than 3 inches may go on the rock, and clean fill sand goes in moisture-conditioned lifts. Landscape-only areas need no compaction testing and may finish with 18 inches of planting soil. Areas meant for future structures need 90% relative compaction and a geotechnical report before final, and no structure may go on the fill without a geotechnical foundation investigation.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

Hauling the debris in Garden Grove

Garden Grove's construction and demolition ordinance requires a waste reduction and recycling plan and a security deposit, $1,000 on the city's form, before the permit, with a report within 60 days of finishing. The city's program guide sets 65% diversion, takes plans online through Green Halo and names Republic Services as the exclusive franchisee for construction bins; self-hauling to an approved facility is the alternative.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Garden Grove's stormwater code lists dechlorinated swimming pool water among allowed discharges, and the countywide H2OC brochure asks for the sanitary sewer when possible, with storm drain discharges limited to 0.1 mg/L of chlorine at a pH of 6.5 to 8.5 and saltwater pools kept off the storm drain.

Common Garden Grove questions

Does a Garden Grove landscape-only fill need compaction testing?
No. IB 26-012 allows tractor rolling or tamping with no compaction testing for landscape-only areas.

Can a Garden Grove homeowner build over a filled pool?
Only with 90% compaction, a geotechnical report and a geotechnical foundation investigation.

How many drainage openings does Garden Grove require?
At least two, each at least 12 inches, with one at the lowest point.

Which Garden Grove pool rules apply, 2020 or 2026?
The 2026 bulletin, IB 26-012; the stricter 2020 policy is still online but older.

Does a Garden Grove pool removal need a debris deposit?
The city's construction and demolition program takes a $1,000 deposit before the permit.
