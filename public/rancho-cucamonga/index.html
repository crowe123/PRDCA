Rancho Cucamonga Pool Removal Permit Rules

Rancho Cucamonga's Building and Safety Services Department sets its pool procedure in form BSF-0027 (Revision C, January 2026), which starts with a site plan showing the pool and its distances to structures and property lines. The city's master fee schedule effective July 2026 lists residential demolition at $490. The form also requires dust control throughout the job and limits work to 7 am to 8 pm, Monday to Saturday, with no Sunday work.

Inspections on a Rancho Cucamonga job

BSF-0027 sets two inspections:

  1. A preparation inspection, which the building inspector must approve before any backfill.
  2. A final inspection, after the decking and driveway are broom-swept, with the soils compaction report ready where one is required.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Rancho Cucamonga

The published method leaves the shell. The coping and 24 inches of wall come off all around, steel exposed during the wall removal is cut off and hauled away, at least 40% of the bottom is removed throughout, and the interior steps and any diving board slab come out. Backfill goes in 24-inch layers, levelled and watered, with 90% compaction certified by a licensed geotechnical engineer, or clean washed sand or 3/8-inch pea gravel with documentation instead of the compaction report, then a final 12 inches of fill raked level. The form does not address full removal or building over the area.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

What a Rancho Cucamonga job costs

No contractor publishes Rancho Cucamonga prices, so statewide figures apply: about $3,500 to $12,000 for a partial fill-in and $8,000 to $30,000 for a full removal. The city's own charge is the $490 demolition fee, and choosing sand or pea gravel over compacted soil removes the geotechnical engineer's fee from the job.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Rancho Cucamonga's stormwater code bans pool water containing chlorine, acids or other chemicals and allows dechlorinated pool water after a non-stormwater discharge notification filed five days ahead with the Engineering Department. The San Bernardino County Stormwater Program adds that chlorine is usually gone after three to five days standing, and the pH should be 6.5 to 8.5.
