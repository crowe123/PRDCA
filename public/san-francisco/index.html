A contractor's licence classes matter on a pool job. San Francisco permits are generally issued to holders of the A (general engineering), C-12 (earthwork and paving), C-21 (building moving and demolition) or C-53 (swimming pool) classes described in the state licence board's classification guide, and the accepted classes differ from city to city. Industry Bulletin 24-07 confirms that the $1,000 unlicensed-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit, so a pool removal always needs a licensed contractor or an owner-builder permit.

San Francisco Pool Removal Permit Rules

San Francisco publishes no rule specific to pool removal. The general requirement in Building Code section 106A applies: a permit is needed to remove any regulated structure, and a swimming pool is one. The Department of Building Inspection is the office to call for the permit type, the fee and any engineering it expects for the fill. Bay Area jobs also need a Bay Area Air Quality Management District notice for demolition work.

Partial fill-in or full removal in San Francisco

With no published pool rule, San Francisco owners should confirm with the Department of Building Inspection whether a partial fill-in is accepted on their lot. Many San Francisco pools sit on tight lots with limited equipment access, and contractors add about $2,000 where clearance is under 6 ft. A full removal with compacted, engineer-certified fill is the safest route where anything will be built later.

A partial fill-in breaks drainage holes through the floor, removes the top of the wall and fills the shell with soil. The method costs less and finishes faster, usually in one to four days, but most cities limit the area to landscaping afterwards. A full removal hauls away every piece of the shell and replaces it with fill compacted in layers to 90%, which takes three to seven days and is the route for anyone planning a structure over the old pool.

Published prices for Bay Area

Souza & Son's publishes $4,500 to $10,000 for a partial fill-in and $8,000 to $22,000 for a full removal of a 15 by 30 ft pool. Bye Bye Pool in Concord quotes $3,500 to $7,000 and $9,000 to $19,000, and Walberg, Inc. gives $5,000 to $12,000 and $12,000 to $30,000 for Northern California.

Four things move a quote more than anything else: equipment access (about $2,000 more where the side yard is under 6 ft wide), deck removal ($500 to $1,700), fill dirt (80 to 150 cubic yards for a full removal) and the engineer's compaction report ($1,500 to $4,000). The California pool removal cost guide sets out every published price by region and pool type.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The San Diego pools and spas fact sheet and LA Stormwater both allow the storm drain only for dechlorinated water within a set pH range, and San Francisco contractors generally follow the same practice. The Department of Building Inspection is the office to ask about the local rule.

Removing a pool can lower the assessed value of a San Francisco property. The Board of Equalization's letter 86/09 treats removal of an improvement as a reason to reduce assessed value, but the reduction is tied to the pool's own assessed value, so an older pool may bring only a small change. Sellers should also expect the question on the state's seller disclosure form, which asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common San Francisco questions

Does San Francisco have a pool removal handout?

No. The general rule in Building Code section 106A requires a permit to remove any regulated structure; the Department of Building Inspection confirms the details.

Does narrow access raise the price in San Francisco?

Contractors add about $2,000 where equipment access is under 6 ft wide, which is common on city lots.

Which licence should a San Francisco contractor hold?

Cities name A, C-12, C-21 or C-53 classes; the state licence board lookup shows each contractor's classes.
