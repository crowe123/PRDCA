San Francisco Pool Removal Permit Rules

San Francisco publishes no rule specific to pool removal. Building Code section 106A requires a separate permit before any regulated building or structure is removed or demolished, and section 106A.1.5 requires a permit for demolishing any structure. The only pools on the exemption list are small prefabricated ones with walls entirely above grade, so an in-ground pool needs a permit. The Building Code fee tables, amended in July 2026, set the demolition permit fee at $724 and standard inspection at $519 an hour, and grading or fill is valued separately by the volume of material handled.

Air district and soil rules in San Francisco

The Bay Area Air Quality Management District asks for notice at least 10 business days before every demolition, with a 72-hour option for homes of four units or fewer. Its rule defines demolition by load-supporting members of a building or facility and does not mention pools, so the district confirms whether a pool-only job counts.

Two city health code rules can reach a pool job. Article 22A, the Maher Ordinance, applies in mapped areas where a project disturbs 50 cubic yards of soil or more, and Article 22B construction dust control applies to projects over half an acre. The site mitigation page shows whether a lot falls in the Maher area.

Partial fill-in or full removal in San Francisco

The Department of Building Inspection's demolition information sheet sends a full building demolition through Form 6 and partial demolition through Form 3/8, and says nothing about pools, so DBI confirms which form a pool removal uses and whether a partial fill-in is accepted. Full removal with compacted fill is the route to discuss on any lot where something may be built later.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

Hauling the debris in San Francisco

San Francisco's construction and demolition rules require debris recycled or reused, with no landfill disposal. Since January 2022, mixed debris may be hauled only by a transporter holding an Environment Department permit for each box or vehicle, unless an owner hauls in their own vehicle, and it must go to a registered facility such as Recology San Francisco on Tunnel Avenue. Covered projects recycle at least 65%, or 75% for a full demolition on Form 6, and file a plan through Green Halo, with fines of up to $1,000 a day.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. San Francisco is the only coastal city in California with a combined sewer system, so most drains carry stormwater and sewage together to treatment. The SFPUC issues batch wastewater discharge permits for occasional and temporary discharges, and it confirms the route and any permit before a pool is emptied.

Sellers in San Francisco should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common San Francisco questions

Does San Francisco require a permit to remove a pool?

Yes. The Building Code requires a permit to demolish any structure, and in-ground pools are not exempt.

What does a San Francisco demolition permit cost?

$724 on the Building Code fee table amended in July 2026.

Where does the concrete from a San Francisco pool go?

To a registered facility; the city bars landfill disposal of construction and demolition debris.

Who can haul pool debris in San Francisco?

A transporter with an Environment Department permit, or the owner in their own vehicle.
