A contractor's licence classes matter on a pool job. Stockton permits are generally issued to holders of the A (general engineering), C-12 (earthwork and paving), C-21 (building moving and demolition) or C-53 (swimming pool) classes described in the state licence board's classification guide, and the accepted classes differ from city to city. Industry Bulletin 24-07 confirms that the $1,000 unlicensed-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit, so a pool removal always needs a licensed contractor or an owner-builder permit.

Stockton Pool Removal Permit Rules

Stockton has one of the best-documented pool permits in this directory. The Community Development Department issues a building permit priced at $97.25 for fiscal year 2026-27, and Form BLDG-0014 sets out two options. Option A leaves the shell with four holes in the bottom and the top two feet of wall removed. Option B removes the pool and replaces it with engineered fill. The form names the A, C-12 and C-53 licence classes, and owner-builders may pull Option A.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Stockton

Stockton's Option A and Option B set out the choice more clearly than any other city. Option A is the cheaper partial fill-in for a yard staying as landscape. Option B, full removal with engineered fill, is the route for anyone planning to build there. Stockton allows pool water to the sewer clean-out only.

A partial fill-in breaks drainage holes through the floor, removes the top of the wall and fills the shell with soil. The method costs less and finishes faster, usually in one to four days, but most cities limit the area to landscaping afterwards. A full removal hauls away every piece of the shell and replaces it with fill compacted in layers to 90%, which takes three to seven days and is the route for anyone planning a structure over the old pool.

Published prices for Sacramento and the Central Valley

No contractor in this part of California publishes local prices, so the statewide figures apply. Northern California firms such as Walberg, Inc. quote $3,500 to $12,000 for a partial fill-in and $8,000 to $30,000 for a full removal, and Los Angeles firms start at $10,500 and $17,500. National guides such as HomeAdvisor run lower and work best as a floor.

Four things move a quote more than anything else: equipment access (about $2,000 more where the side yard is under 6 ft wide), deck removal ($500 to $1,700), fill dirt (80 to 150 cubic yards for a full removal) and the engineer's compaction report ($1,500 to $4,000). The California pool removal cost guide sets out every published price by region and pool type.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Stockton and Riverside allow pool water to the sewer clean-out only, the San Diego pools and spas fact sheet and LA Stormwater both allow the storm drain only for dechlorinated water within a set pH range, and Stockton contractors generally follow the same practice. The Community Development Department, Building and Life Safety Division is the office to ask about the local rule.

Removing a pool can lower the assessed value of a Stockton property. The Board of Equalization's letter 86/09 treats removal of an improvement as a reason to reduce assessed value, but the reduction is tied to the pool's own assessed value, so an older pool may bring only a small change. Sellers should also expect the question on the state's seller disclosure form, which asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.
