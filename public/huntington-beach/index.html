Huntington Beach Pool Removal Permit Rules

Huntington Beach requires a demolition permit before any pool demolition work, and the Department of Community Development's pool demolition guidelines set the procedure. A homeowner, a B general contractor, a C-53 pool contractor or a C-21 demolition contractor may apply, with a plot plan showing the pool and its setbacks and a preliminary report from a licensed soils engineer describing how the pool will be demolished or abandoned. The city's 2026 fee schedule charges a $41 processing fee plus an inspection fee based on project valuation, starting at $85.

Inspections on a Huntington Beach job

The guidelines list three inspections, booked through the city's online portal or at (714) 536-5241:

  1. Bottom and bond beam removal.
  2. Disconnection and removal of the electrical lighting, equipment feeds and gas line.
  3. Final inspection of the finished grade, with the soils compaction report submitted.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Huntington Beach

The guidelines describe leaving the shell bottom in place. Crews break out or puncture holes in the bottom so water drains, remove all decking and the bond beam to within 12 inches below grade, and remove all electrical conduit and gas lines. At least 6 inches of 3/4-inch gravel goes across the bottom, then dirt compacted in 12-inch lifts to finished grade, with a soils compaction report from a licensed soils engineer showing at least 90%. The guidelines do not address building over the area later, so that question goes to the Building Division before work starts.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

Hauling the debris in Huntington Beach

Republic Services is the only trash collector allowed to operate in Huntington Beach, and bins from unauthorized haulers are cited or impounded, according to the city's construction debris handout. Republic offers 10-yard low-boy boxes for concrete and dirt, at (714) 847-3581, and self-haul debris goes to Rainbow Environmental on Nichols Lane, now part of Republic. The city applies CALGreen's construction debris rules through municipal code chapter 17.52.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Huntington Beach's stormwater ordinance exempts swimming pool water dechlorinated to 0.1 ppm or less, bans cleaning wastewater and filter backwash, and counts pool water containing chlorine or other chemicals as a pollutant. The countywide H2OC brochure asks for the sanitary sewer when possible, a pH of 6.5 to 8.5, and saltwater pools kept off the storm drain.

Sellers in Huntington Beach should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common Huntington Beach questions

Does a Huntington Beach pool removal need a soils engineer?
Yes. The guidelines require a preliminary soils report up front and a compaction report showing at least 90% at the end.

How deep does Huntington Beach cut the pool walls?
Decking and the bond beam come off to within 12 inches below grade.

How many inspections does a Huntington Beach pool removal need?
Three: bottom and bond beam removal, utility disconnection, and the final with the compaction report.
