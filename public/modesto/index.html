Modesto Pool Removal Permit Rules

Modesto publishes no rule specific to pool removal. The city's building fee schedule for July 2026 to June 2027 lists a $301 demolition permit and a separate $550 line for swimming pools of all types, without saying which covers a removal, so Building Safety on 10th Street (209-577-5232) confirms the permit before work starts. Applications and inspection requests go through the city's eTRAKiT portal. The municipal code's section on demolishing buildings requires a bond filed with the City Clerk and notice to the utilities so gas, electric, sewer and water lines can be capped.

Air district and code rules in Modesto

Modesto sits in the San Joaquin Valley Air Pollution Control District. The district's Demolition Permit Release form must be signed and returned to the building department before a demolition permit is issued. The form is written for structures, so Building Safety confirms whether a pool-only job needs it. The district's Rule 8021 on dust from demolition and excavation calls for a dust control plan only on residential sites of 10 acres or more or on work moving over 2,500 cubic yards a day, far above a backyard pool.

The municipal code also lists abandoned swimming pools and spas among attractive nuisances, so an empty pool left open can be treated as a nuisance under the code.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Modesto

Without a pool rule, the code's general demolition section is the closest guide: it requires excavations filled with dirt or sand to street level within 10 days. A swimming pool covered by a valid building permit needs no separate grading and erosion control permit. Owners should ask Building Safety whether a partial fill-in is accepted and whether the area is then limited to landscaping, and nearby Stockton's published options show what a Central Valley inspector may expect.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

What a Modesto job costs

No contractor publishes Modesto prices, so statewide figures apply: about $3,500 to $12,000 for a partial fill-in and $8,000 to $30,000 for a full removal. Modesto's own charges add the $301 demolition or $550 pool permit on the 2026-27 schedule, and the city adds 2.4% to card payments.

Hauling the debris in Modesto

Modesto's municipal code counts rock, concrete, asphalt and dirt from permitted building or demolition work as construction and demolition debris. The city holds exclusive service agreements with Bertolotti Disposal Service and Gilton Solid Waste Management, and the code lets businesses doing demolition or construction cleanup remove the rubbish and salvage their own work produces. The city adopted the 2025 CALGreen code in June 2026, which sets a 65% diversion target on the projects it covers.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. In Modesto it never goes to the street or storm drain, even when dechlorinated: the city's stormwater guidance and municipal code send it to the sanitary sewer through the yard's sewer cleanout, and Environmental Services takes reports of illegal discharges at 209-577-6200.

Sellers in Modesto should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common Modesto questions

Does Modesto publish pool removal rules?

No pool demolition handout exists. The 2026-27 schedule lists a $301 demolition permit and a $550 pool permit, and Building Safety confirms which applies.

Can Modesto pool water go to the storm drain?

No. Even dechlorinated pool water goes to the sanitary sewer, usually through the yard's sewer cleanout.

Who hauls pool debris in Modesto?

Bertolotti Disposal Service and Gilton Solid Waste Management hold the city's service agreements, and demolition firms may remove the debris their own work produces.
