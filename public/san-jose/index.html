A contractor's licence classes matter on a pool job. San Jose permits are generally issued to holders of the A (general engineering), C-12 (earthwork and paving), C-21 (building moving and demolition) or C-53 (swimming pool) classes described in the state licence board's classification guide, and the accepted classes differ from city to city. Industry Bulletin 24-07 confirms that the $1,000 unlicensed-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit, so a pool removal always needs a licensed contractor or an owner-builder permit.

San Jose Pool Removal Permit Rules

San Jose's Development Services Permit Center handles pool demolition on a building permit, and the city's swimming pool demolition page (Bulletin #289) lists the requirements. The city takes a refundable debris deposit rather than publishing a flat pool fee. The filled area is marked non-buildable unless an engineer certifies the compaction, and the job needs a job number from the Bay Area Air Quality Management District before demolition starts. San Jose can issue the permit in a single visit, which shortens the usual wait.

Partial fill-in or full removal in San Jose

San Jose's choice turns on the non-buildable marking. A partial fill-in without an engineer's compaction certificate leaves the area recorded as non-buildable, which limits later plans for an accessory dwelling unit. A full removal with certified compaction clears that marking. Santa Clara County owners turning the pool into low-water landscape may also qualify for the Valley Water landscape rebate of $2 a square foot, up to $3,000, with approval before work starts.

A partial fill-in breaks drainage holes through the floor, removes the top of the wall and fills the shell with soil. The method costs less and finishes faster, usually in one to four days, but most cities limit the area to landscaping afterwards. A full removal hauls away every piece of the shell and replaces it with fill compacted in layers to 90%, which takes three to seven days and is the route for anyone planning a structure over the old pool.

Published prices for Bay Area

Souza & Son's publishes $4,500 to $10,000 for a partial fill-in and $8,000 to $22,000 for a full removal of a 15 by 30 ft pool. Bye Bye Pool in Concord quotes $3,500 to $7,000 and $9,000 to $19,000, and Walberg, Inc. gives $5,000 to $12,000 and $12,000 to $30,000 for Northern California.

Four things move a quote more than anything else: equipment access (about $2,000 more where the side yard is under 6 ft wide), deck removal ($500 to $1,700), fill dirt (80 to 150 cubic yards for a full removal) and the engineer's compaction report ($1,500 to $4,000). The California pool removal cost guide sets out every published price by region and pool type.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The San Diego pools and spas fact sheet and LA Stormwater both allow the storm drain only for dechlorinated water within a set pH range, and San Jose contractors generally follow the same practice. The Building Division, Development Services Permit Center is the office to ask about the local rule.

Removing a pool can lower the assessed value of a San Jose property. The Board of Equalization's letter 86/09 treats removal of an improvement as a reason to reduce assessed value, but the reduction is tied to the pool's own assessed value, so an older pool may bring only a small change. Sellers should also expect the question on the state's seller disclosure form, which asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common San Jose questions

Is a filled pool area buildable in San Jose?

Only when an engineer certifies the compaction; otherwise the city marks the area non-buildable.

Does San Jose pool removal need an air district number?

Yes. The job needs a Bay Area Air Quality Management District job number before demolition starts.

Is there a rebate for removing a pool in San Jose?

Valley Water pays $2 a square foot, up to $3,000, when a pool becomes low-water landscape, with approval before work begins.
