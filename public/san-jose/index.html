San Jose Pool Removal Permit Rules

San Jose requires a building permit before a pool or spa is removed or abandoned, and the city's swimming pool demolition page, updated April 2026, sets the steps. For qualifying properties the permit can be issued during a single visit if the plans and documents are accurate, arriving before 3 pm. Before applying, the owner needs a job number from the Bay Area Air Quality Management District, whether or not asbestos is involved. The city's 2026-27 fee schedule has no pool demolition line; residential exterior and site alterations, which include pools, carry $211 for issuance, $325 for plan review and $367 for inspection.

Paperwork on a San Jose job

The city's Bulletin 289 requires the gas line and electrical wiring capped, which the inspector verifies, with the breaker relabelled as a spare. A buildable-area job also needs a special inspection form naming who will observe the compaction, unless the engineer of record inspects continuously. A permit card lists the inspections, booked at SJPermits.org or 408-535-3555, and a debris box kept on the sidewalk or street needs a revocable encroachment permit. Community pools and properties on the Historic Resources Inventory need Planning clearance.

Partial fill-in or full removal in San Jose

San Jose asks the owner to choose up front. For an area that will not be built on, the plot plan marks the non-buildable area with its dimensions and shows what replaces the pool, and the inspector collects the plan. For a buildable area, a state-licensed engineer prepares and wet-stamps a demolition, drainage and compaction plan and a plot plan, the whole shell and its rebar are removed, and the fill reaches at least 90% of maximum dry density, with a compaction report declaring the area buildable.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

Hauling the debris in San Jose

San Jose's Construction and Demolition Diversion Deposit applies to all residential demolitions and is collected when the permit is issued, at $0.35 a square foot. The deposit comes back when at least 50% of the debris is diverted at a city-certified facility, self-hauled or carried by a franchised debris box company, with weight tickets showing the address or permit number and a refund request through Waste Tracking San Jose within 365 days of the final inspection.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. San Jose's pool guidance says not to drain to a street, gutter or storm drain and to send the water to a sanitary sewer clean-out, naming copper and chlorine as the pollutants of concern. Environmental Services answers questions at (408) 945-3000.

Sellers in San Jose should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common San Jose questions

Can a San Jose pool removal permit be issued in one visit?

Yes for qualifying properties, if the plans and documents are accurate and the owner arrives before 3 pm.

Does a San Jose pool removal need an air district job number?

Yes. The city requires a Bay Area Air Quality Management District job number before applying.

What makes a San Jose pool area buildable?

Engineer-stamped plans, full removal of the shell and rebar, and a compaction report at 90%.

How much is the San Jose debris deposit?

$0.35 a square foot for residential demolition, refunded with 50% diversion.

Does Valley Water pay to convert a pool to landscape?

Its landscape conversion rebate covers pools at $2 a square foot, up to $3,000 for most homes, but only with written approval before removal starts.
