Bakersfield Pool Removal Permit Rules

Bakersfield publishes no rule specific to pool removal. The city's building permit application has separate boxes for pool and spa work and for demolition, without saying which covers a removal, so the Development Services Building Division on Chester Avenue (661-326-3720) confirms the permit type. The city's fee schedule of 1 July 2026 charges a building permit at $291 minimum plus an amount based on project valuation, with no separate line for demolition or pools.

Air district rules in Bakersfield

Bakersfield sits in the San Joaquin Valley Air Pollution Control District, which requires a Demolition Permit Release form from the district before a building official issues a demolition permit, and asbestos notification at least 10 working days ahead of demolition. Both are written for structures, so the Building Division confirms whether a pool-only job needs them. The district's Rule 8021 requires a dust control plan only on residential sites of 10 acres or more or on work moving over 2,500 cubic yards a day, far above a backyard pool.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Bakersfield

Without a published rule, Bakersfield owners should ask the Building Division whether a partial fill-in is accepted and whether the area is then limited to landscaping, as most California cities require. Full removal with compacted, certified fill keeps the yard buildable.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

What a Bakersfield job costs

No contractor publishes Bakersfield prices, so statewide figures apply: about $3,500 to $12,000 for a partial fill-in and $8,000 to $30,000 for a full removal. Bakersfield's own charges add the $291 minimum building permit plus valuation and the haul: a city 15 cubic yard bin for concrete or dirt costs $149 to deliver and $369.45 a haul before the county gate fee of $95.65 a ton.

Hauling the debris in Bakersfield

Bakersfield limits waste collection to collectors licensed by city contract or franchise, and the municipal code leaves contractors paid to remove debris out of its self-haul definition. The city's roll-off service offers a 15 cubic yard bin for dirt, concrete, asphalt or heavy material, limited to 10 tons a load, on the 2026-27 rate sheet. The city's Mount Vernon recycling facility does not take dirt, and its facility handout points to the Bena Landfill on Neumarkel Road for concrete and demolition debris, priced on Kern County's gate fee list, and to Bowman Asphalt and Sturgeon & Son, which take concrete, asphalt and clean dirt at no charge.

The city uses the state CW-1 construction waste management form, which carries the CALGreen target of at least 65% diversion.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. Bakersfield's sewer code bars swimming pool drainage from the public sewer unless the city engineer specifically authorizes it, and its stormwater code bans unsanitary or harmful matter from the storm system. The city publishes no pool-draining guidance, so the Building Division or the city engineer's office confirms the route before a pool is emptied.

Sellers in Bakersfield should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.
