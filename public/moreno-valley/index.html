A contractor's licence classes matter on a pool job. Moreno Valley permits are generally issued to holders of the A (general engineering), C-12 (earthwork and paving), C-21 (building moving and demolition) or C-53 (swimming pool) classes described in the state licence board's classification guide, and the accepted classes differ from city to city. Industry Bulletin 24-07 confirms that the $1,000 unlicensed-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit, so a pool removal always needs a licensed contractor or an owner-builder permit.

Moreno Valley Pool Removal Permit Rules

Moreno Valley's Building and Safety Division issues a demolition permit for pools and spas, and the city's pool and spa demolition handout (2022) sets out two options. The general demolition fee line for 2026-27 is $153 for plan check and $347 for inspection; the schedule has no pool-specific line. Option A leaves the shell: three holes of at least 2 ft go through the bottom, at least 12 inches of #4 rock is placed, the coping and bond beam come off to at least 18 inches below grade, and the fill is compacted to 90% under a geotechnical consultant. Option B removes the whole shell and backfills with engineered fill in lifts of no more than 8 inches, with a stamped geotechnical report. Every job has at least two inspections.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Moreno Valley

Moreno Valley ties Option A to a signed acknowledgement: the owner agrees to keep the area as open space, landscape or garden, and to disclose it on sale. Option B is required if anything will be built on the area. Both options need a geotechnical consultant, so the engineering cost sits in every Moreno Valley job.

A partial fill-in breaks drainage holes through the floor, removes the top of the wall and fills the shell with soil. The method costs less and finishes faster, usually in one to four days, but most cities limit the area to landscaping afterwards. A full removal hauls away every piece of the shell and replaces it with fill compacted in layers to 90%, which takes three to seven days and is the route for anyone planning a structure over the old pool.

Published prices for Inland Empire

No contractor in this part of California publishes local prices, so the statewide figures apply. Northern California firms such as Walberg, Inc. quote $3,500 to $12,000 for a partial fill-in and $8,000 to $30,000 for a full removal, and Los Angeles firms start at $10,500 and $17,500. National guides such as HomeAdvisor run lower and work best as a floor.

Four things move a quote more than anything else: equipment access (about $2,000 more where the side yard is under 6 ft wide), deck removal ($500 to $1,700), fill dirt (80 to 150 cubic yards for a full removal) and the engineer's compaction report ($1,500 to $4,000). The California pool removal cost guide sets out every published price by region and pool type.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The San Diego pools and spas fact sheet and LA Stormwater both allow the storm drain only for dechlorinated water within a set pH range, and Moreno Valley contractors generally follow the same practice. The Community Development Department, Building and Safety Division confirms the local rule.

Removing a pool can lower the assessed value of a Moreno Valley property. The Board of Equalization's letter 86/09 treats removal of an improvement as a reason to reduce assessed value, but the reduction is tied to the pool's own assessed value, so an older pool may bring only a small change. Sellers should also expect the question on the state's seller disclosure form, which asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.
