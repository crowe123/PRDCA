A contractor's licence classes matter on a pool job. Sacramento permits are generally issued to holders of the A (general engineering), C-12 (earthwork and paving), C-21 (building moving and demolition) or C-53 (swimming pool) classes described in the state licence board's classification guide, and the accepted classes differ from city to city. Industry Bulletin 24-07 confirms that the $1,000 unlicensed-work exemption does not cover work that needs a permit, so a pool removal always needs a licensed contractor or an owner-builder permit.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Sacramento

Without a published pool rule, the Sacramento choice depends on later plans. NorCal Earthworks writes about full removal ahead of an accessory dwelling unit, which is the common reason for taking the whole shell out. A partial fill-in suits a yard staying as lawn or garden, subject to what the department accepts.

A partial fill-in breaks drainage holes through the floor, removes the top of the wall and fills the shell with soil. The method costs less and finishes faster, usually in one to four days, but most cities limit the area to landscaping afterwards. A full removal hauls away every piece of the shell and replaces it with fill compacted in layers to 90%, which takes three to seven days and is the route for anyone planning a structure over the old pool.

Published prices for Sacramento and the Central Valley

NorCal Earthworks in Sacramento prices full removal at $8,000 to $15,000 for a concrete pool and $6,000 to $12,000 for fiberglass. Walberg, Inc. publishes $5,000 to $12,000 for a partial fill-in and $12,000 to $30,000 for a full removal across Northern California.

Four things move a quote more than anything else: equipment access (about $2,000 more where the side yard is under 6 ft wide), deck removal ($500 to $1,700), fill dirt (80 to 150 cubic yards for a full removal) and the engineer's compaction report ($1,500 to $4,000). The California pool removal cost guide sets out every published price by region and pool type.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The San Diego pools and spas fact sheet and LA Stormwater both allow the storm drain only for dechlorinated water within a set pH range, and Sacramento contractors generally follow the same practice. The Community Development Department, Building Division confirms the local rule.

Removing a pool can lower the assessed value of a Sacramento property. The Board of Equalization's letter 86/09 treats removal of an improvement as a reason to reduce assessed value, but the reduction is tied to the pool's own assessed value, so an older pool may bring only a small change. Sellers should also expect the question on the state's seller disclosure form, which asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.
