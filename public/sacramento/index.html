Sacramento Pool Removal Permit Rules

Sacramento publishes no rule specific to pool removal. City Code chapter 15.44 requires a wrecking permit from the building official before any demolition, and the city's pool requirements describe pools as structures, so the wrecking permit is the likely route; the Building Division confirms it. The wrecking permit application CDD-0233 (revised September 2026) needs sign-off from Planning, Fire, Solid Waste and Utilities before issue and asks whether excavations on the lot will be filled or fenced. The city's building fee detail, effective July 2025, sets the wrecking permit at $175, and water taps cost $274 each to kill or $2,005 each to abandon.

Air district notice in Sacramento

The Sacramento Metropolitan Air Quality Management District treats demolitions of any size as needing notification, with a 10-day notice period and a standard fee of $435. Its rule defines demolition by load-supporting members of a facility, and its FAQ says single-family homes are usually not regulated, so the district rules on whether a pool-only job counts.

Partial fill-in or full removal in Sacramento

Without a pool rule, the city code's wrecking section is the closest guide: excavations must be filled level with the adjoining grade within 10 days after the work, inorganic fill such as broken masonry is allowed, and the top foot of fill must be clean earth. The city's pool requirements also warn that expansive soils are likely at any Sacramento site, which is worth raising with the Building Division before choosing between a partial fill-in and full removal.

The fill-in questions page sets the two methods side by side, with typical timelines of one to four days for a partial fill-in and three to seven for a full removal.

Hauling the debris in Sacramento

Since 15 January 2026, Sacramento's wrecking permit applicants submit a Waste Management Plan in Green Halo, upload disposal tickets and file a waste log within 30 days of finishing. Debris may be hauled only by a franchised waste hauler or self-hauled, and mixed loads go to a county-certified sorting facility such as GreenWaste Florin Perkins, L and D Landfill or Sierra Waste. The city's recycling rules require 65% diversion on all demolition projects, with an administration fee of 0.04% of valuation, from $40 to $800.

Pool water has to go somewhere before demolition starts. The Sacramento Stormwater Quality Partnership pool guidance hosted by the city sends it to the sanitary sewer through a sewer cleanout or laundry sink, never an opened manhole, and allows the storm drain only for water below 0.01 parts per million of chlorine, with a pH of 6.5 to 8.5, no debris and no heat. Saltwater pools go to the sewer only, and Regional San at (916) 875-6470 handles sewer questions.

Sellers in Sacramento should keep the finaled permit, because the state's seller disclosure form asks about fill on the property and about unpermitted work.

Common Sacramento questions

Which permit covers a Sacramento pool removal?
Most likely the $175 wrecking permit under City Code chapter 15.44; the Building Division confirms it.

How soon must a Sacramento pool be filled?
City code requires excavations filled level with the adjoining grade within 10 days, with the top foot clean earth.

Can Sacramento pool water go to the storm drain?
Only below 0.01 ppm chlorine at pH 6.5 to 8.5; the sewer cleanout is the preferred route.

Who hauls pool debris in Sacramento?
A franchised waste hauler, or the contractor self-hauling, with a Waste Management Plan in Green Halo.
